Research Purpose

This research aims to establish a new interdisciplinary field called "Materials Process Data Science" by integrating data science with materials process engineering.

Specifically, we are promoting the following research:

  • Comprehensively construct a materials-process integrated database for nanoparticle synthesis
  • Extract process characteristic factors that determine material structure and function from the constructed database
  • Establish materials process informatics as a guideline for creating high-performance nanomaterials

This will accelerate next-generation nanomaterial development and revolutionize materials process engineering.

Current Results

Where the research stands today, mapped from the research purpose to equipment, papers, observed results and the next challenges (as of October 2026). The news items give the background and the papers give the full references.

MOF particle-size control with a low-cost automated synthesis system

A two-solution mixing system built from a robotic arm (about 200,000 yen) and an electric pipette synthesises ZIF-8, a representative MOF, while logging injection volume, injection speed, temperature and mass.

Observed results
ZIF-8 formation was confirmed by XRD at injection times of 5, 2 and 0.5 s. Within the compared conditions, DLS showed that slower injection tended to give larger particles.
Related paper and talk
Y. Wang, H. Liu, Y. Hashimoto, K. Iwase, H. Li, T. Tomai, Chem. Sci. 17, 15034 (2026) DOI / SCEJ 57th Autumn Meeting (September 2026)
Next challenge
Link the logged operation data with the resulting properties and move on to machine-learning property prediction and optimisation of synthesis conditions.

An AI materials map integrating experimental and computational data

Graph-based machine learning integrates experimental data with theoretical calculation data and visualises the structural similarity of materials as a two-dimensional map.

Observed results
The message-passing neural network captured the structural complexity of materials and organised their placement on the map, but this did not necessarily translate into better property-prediction accuracy.
Paper and press
Y. Hashimoto, X. Jia, H. Li, T. Tomai, APL Mach. Learn. 3, 036104 (2025) DOI / Tohoku University press release (July 31, 2025)
Next challenge
Connect the map with the materials-process database set out in the research purpose, so that synthesis conditions become part of the map.

Autonomous analysis of an experimental database by multi-agent AI

On Claude Code we built a three-layer, nine-agent system whose agents share data extraction, machine learning, SHAP analysis, knowledge-base construction and review, and which analyses the Starrydata2 thermoelectric database repeatedly.

Observed results
417 projects over 100 topics generated about 8,110 knowledge-base items automatically. Network analysis of the knowledge base and comparison of knowledge maps obtained with different numbers of topics and cycles showed that inheriting knowledge across cycles expands the exploration to related materials and deepens it.
Talks
Society of Data-driven Materials Science (July 2026) / Agentic AI Summit 2026 (August 2026) / JSAP Autumn Meeting (September 2026)
Next challenges
Quantitative evaluation of the generated knowledge, computational cost and token limits, countermeasures against hallucination, and research databases that AI can use directly.

September 14, 2026

Research Presentation at the SCEJ 57th Autumn Meeting

Associate Professor Hashimoto gave an oral presentation in the session "Frontiers of Data-Driven Research and Development" at the Society of Chemical Engineers, Japan (SCEJ) 57th Autumn Meeting, Hiroshima University.

Presentation Title: Development of an Automated MOF Synthesis System Using a Robotic Arm and an Electric Pipette

The presentation introduced a two-solution mixing automated experimental system combining an electric pipette and a robotic arm, a systematic evaluation of how precursor injection speed affects ZIF-8 particle size, and the path toward quantitative control and improved reproducibility of synthesis conditions.

Research Presentation at the 87th JSAP Autumn Meeting

Associate Professor Hashimoto gave an oral presentation at the 87th JSAP Autumn Meeting 2026 (Japan Society of Applied Physics), Hokkaido University.

Presentation Title: Expanding and Deepening Autonomous Exploration of Physical Property Information from Materials Experimental Databases Using Multi-Agent AI

The presentation reported autonomous data-analysis experiments on the Starrydata2 thermoelectric materials database with systematically varied numbers of topics and cycles, showing through knowledge-map comparison that running multiple topics and cycles with inherited knowledge expands and deepens the exploration of property knowledge latent in the database.

Presentation Title: Autonomous Multi-Agent AI System for Thermoelectric Materials Research and Knowledge System Construction

July 6, 2026

Research Presentation at the Society of Data-driven Materials Science 2026 Annual Meeting

Associate Professor Hashimoto gave an oral presentation at the 2026 Annual Meeting of the Society of Data-driven Materials Science, Institute of Science Tokyo.

Presentation Title: Automation of Thermoelectric Materials Research and Knowledge System Construction by a Multi-Agent AI System

The presentation introduced a three-layer, nine-agent multi-agent AI system implemented on Claude Code, whose agents share data extraction, machine-learning modeling, SHAP analysis and knowledge-base construction; it sustained a long-running autonomous analysis loop on the Starrydata2 thermoelectric database, and network analysis of the resulting knowledge base confirmed deepening and systematization of the knowledge.

July 31, 2025

Paper Published in APL Machine Learning

Associate Professor Hashimoto and research team published a paper on creating "materials maps" using AI in the international journal APL Machine Learning. The paper was published online on July 28, 2025, and Tohoku University issued a press release on July 31, 2025.

This research developed a materials map integrating experimental and theoretical calculation data using message-passing graph neural networks. The map visually represents structural similarity of materials, enabling rapid extraction of unknown high-performance materials.
