## 3.1 Supercritical Carbon Dioxide (sc-CO₂)

### Critical Properties

Supercritical CO₂ is the workhorse of SCF technology due to its favorable critical point:

- **Critical Temperature (Tc)**: 31.1°C (304.3 K)
- **Critical Pressure (Pc)**: 7.38 MPa (72.8 atm)
- **Critical Density (ρc)**: 467.6 kg/m³

### Why sc-CO₂ Dominates Industrial Applications

**1. Mild Critical Conditions**
- Tc near room temperature → low energy requirements
- Accessible with standard equipment
- Suitable for heat-sensitive compounds (pharmaceuticals, natural products)

**2. Safety and Environmental Profile**
- Non-toxic (GRAS: Generally Recognized As Safe)
- Non-flammable (unlike organic solvents)
- No harmful residues in products
- Natural atmospheric component → minimal environmental impact
- Easy separation: depressurization → CO₂ gas leaves product

**3. Economic Advantages**
- Inexpensive raw material (industrial CO₂ from fermentation, ammonia synthesis)
- Recyclable in closed-loop systems
- Reduces waste disposal costs vs. organic solvents

**4. Tunable Properties**
- Density range: 200–900 kg/m³ (gas-like to liquid-like)
- Solvating power adjustable via pressure and temperature
- Polarity tunable with modifiers (ethanol, methanol)

### Density-Pressure-Temperature Relationships

The density of sc-CO₂ varies dramatically near the critical point:

| Temperature (°C) | Pressure (MPa) | Density (kg/m³) | State |
|------------------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| 25              | 5.0            | ~850           | Liquid |
| 31              | 7.0            | ~600           | Supercritical |
| 31.1            | 7.38           | 467.6          | Critical point |
| 40              | 10.0           | ~700           | Supercritical |
| 40              | 20.0           | ~900           | Supercritical |
| 50              | 10.0           | ~500           | Supercritical |

**Key Insight**: Small pressure changes near Tc cause large density changes → highly tunable solvent power.

### Solvent Properties

**Polarity**: sc-CO₂ is **non-polar to slightly polar**
- Quadrupole moment allows weak polar interactions
- Similar to hexane or pentane in liquid phase
- Poor solvent for highly polar compounds (salts, proteins) without modifiers

**Modifier Effects**:
- Adding 5–15 mol% ethanol or methanol increases polarity
- Enables extraction of phenolics, alkaloids, and other polar compounds
- Trade-off: modifier must be separated from product

### Industrial Applications

| Application | Conditions | Advantages |
|-------------|-----------|------------|
| **Coffee/Tea Decaffeination** | 40–90°C, 10–30 MPa | Selective caffeine extraction, no residue |
| **Hop Extraction (Beer)** | 40–50°C, 10–20 MPa | Preserves aroma compounds |
| **Natural Product Extraction** | 40–80°C, 10–35 MPa | Heat-sensitive actives (vitamins, antioxidants) |
| **Dry Cleaning** | 40–60°C, 10–20 MPa | Replaces perchloroethylene |
| **Aerogel Production** | 40–80°C, 10–20 MPa | Supercritical drying preserves nanostructure |
| **RESS (Rapid Expansion)** | 35–80°C, 10–30 MPa | Nanoparticle/microparticle formation |

## 3.2 Supercritical Water (SCW)

### Critical Properties

Water's high critical point creates extreme conditions:

- **Critical Temperature (Tc)**: 374°C (647 K)
- **Critical Pressure (Pc)**: 22.1 MPa (218 atm)
- **Critical Density (ρc)**: 322 kg/m³

### Extreme Property Changes

**1. Dielectric Constant (ε)**

Water's polarity collapses in the supercritical state:

| Temperature (°C) | Pressure (MPa) | Dielectric Constant (ε) |
|------------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| 25              | 0.1            | 78                     |
| 200             | 25             | ~35                    |
| 374             | 22.1           | ~6                     |
| 400             | 25             | ~2                     |

**Consequence**: SCW becomes **non-polar**, dissolving oils, organics, and gases while precipitating salts.

**2. Ion Product (Kw)**

The self-ionization equilibrium shifts dramatically:

- Ambient water (25°C): Kw = 10⁻¹⁴ (pH = 7.0)
- Near-critical (350°C, 25 MPa): Kw ≈ 10⁻¹¹ → higher ion concentration
- Supercritical (400°C, 25 MPa): Kw ≈ 10⁻²⁰ → almost no ions

**3. Viscosity and Diffusivity**

- Viscosity drops: 0.89 mPa·s (25°C) → 0.03–0.05 mPa·s (400°C, 25 MPa)
- Diffusivity increases 10–100× → faster mass transfer
- Gas-like transport in liquid-like density

### Oxidation Chemistry in SCW

**Supercritical Water Oxidation (SCWO)**:
- Add O₂ or H₂O₂ to SCW at 400–650°C, 25–30 MPa
- Organic waste → CO₂ + H₂O + harmless salts
- Complete destruction of toxic organics (PCBs, pesticides, chemical weapons)
- Self-sustaining for concentrated waste (exothermic combustion)

**Reaction Example**:
```
C₆H₅OH + 7 O₂ → 6 CO₂ + 3 H₂O  (phenol oxidation)
```

### Applications

| Application | Conditions | Purpose |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| **SCWO** | 450–650°C, 25–30 MPa | Hazardous waste destruction |
| **Hydrothermal Synthesis** | 200–400°C, 10–40 MPa | Nanoparticle synthesis (ZnO, TiO₂) |
| **Biomass Conversion** | 300–400°C, 15–25 MPa | Hydrothermal liquefaction to bio-oil |
| **Geochemical Processes** | 374–500°C, 22–50 MPa | Mineral formation, ore deposition |

**Safety Note**: SCW requires specialized equipment (corrosion-resistant alloys, high-pressure vessels). Salt precipitation can clog reactors.

## 3.3 Supercritical Ethanol

### Critical Properties

- **Critical Temperature (Tc)**: 241°C (514 K)
- **Critical Pressure (Pc)**: 6.14 MPa (60.6 atm)
- **Critical Density (ρc)**: 276 kg/m³

### Characteristics

**Polarity**: Intermediate (between CO₂ and water)
- Can dissolve polar and non-polar compounds
- Hydrogen bonding capability retained partially

**Green Solvent Credentials**:
- Renewable source (bioethanol from biomass)
- Non-toxic, biodegradable
- Lower critical pressure than water → easier operation

### Applications

| Application | Advantages |
|-------------|------------|
| **Natural Product Extraction** | Better recovery of polar compounds than CO₂ (e.g., polyphenols, glycosides) |
| **Biodiesel Production** | Simultaneous esterification and transesterification at 250–300°C |
| **Pharmaceutical Processing** | Particle formation, purification of heat-sensitive APIs |
| **Biomass Delignification** | Lignin removal for cellulosic ethanol production |

**Example**: Supercritical ethanol biodiesel production
- No catalyst needed (unlike alkaline transesterification)
- Single-phase reaction → faster kinetics
- Tolerates high free fatty acids
- Typical conditions: 250–350°C, 10–20 MPa, 1–10 min

## 3.4 Other Supercritical Fluids

### Supercritical Propane

- **Critical Properties**: Tc = 96.7°C, Pc = 4.25 MPa
- **Polarity**: Non-polar (similar to CO₂)
- **Advantages**: Lower Pc than CO₂, good for lipid extraction
- **Disadvantages**: Flammable → requires safety measures
- **Applications**: Vegetable oil extraction, deasphalting of petroleum

### Supercritical Nitrogen

- **Critical Properties**: Tc = -147°C (-210 K), Pc = 3.4 MPa
- **Polarity**: Non-polar
- **Applications**: Cryogenic extraction, inert atmosphere processing
- **Challenge**: Very low Tc requires cryogenic equipment

### Supercritical Xenon

- **Critical Properties**: Tc = 16.6°C, Pc = 5.84 MPa
- **Advantages**: Very low Tc, non-toxic, inert
- **Disadvantages**: Expensive ($5,000–$10,000 per kg)
- **Applications**: Specialized pharmaceutical processing, radiopharmaceuticals

### Fluorinated Compounds

- **Examples**: R-134a (1,1,1,2-tetrafluoroethane), SF₆
- **Tc range**: 100–200°C
- **Applications**: Specialized cleaning, refrigeration industry
- **Concerns**: Greenhouse gas potential → limited use

## 3.5 Solvent Selection Guide

### Decision Tree for SCF Selection

```
[START]
   |
   V
Is target compound heat-sensitive (Tmax < 100°C)?
   |
   +--YES--> Use CO₂ (Tc = 31°C)
   |
   +--NO---> Is target highly polar or ionic?
              |
              +--YES--> Consider Water (Tc = 374°C)
              |         or Ethanol (Tc = 241°C) with modifiers
              |
              +--NO---> Is flammability acceptable?
                        |
                        +--YES--> Propane or Ethanol
                        |
                        +--NO---> CO₂ or Nitrogen
```

### Comparison Table of Common SCFs

| SCF | Tc (°C) | Pc (MPa) | Polarity | Safety | Cost | Typical Applications |
|-----|---------|----------|----------|--------|------|---------------------|
| **CO₂** | 31.1 | 7.38 | Low (tunable) | Excellent | Low | Extraction, cleaning, RESS |
| **Water** | 374 | 22.1 | High → Low | Good | Very Low | Oxidation, synthesis, biomass |
| **Ethanol** | 241 | 6.14 | Medium | Good | Medium | Biodiesel, polar extraction |
| **Propane** | 96.7 | 4.25 | Low | Flammable | Low | Oil extraction, deasphalting |
| **Nitrogen** | -147 | 3.4 | Very Low | Excellent | Low | Cryogenic, inert processing |
| **Xenon** | 16.6 | 5.84 | Low | Excellent | Very High | Pharma (specialized) |

### Modifier (Co-Solvent) Usage

**Purpose**: Enhance polarity or selectivity of non-polar SCFs (mainly CO₂)

**Common Modifiers**:
- **Ethanol (5–15 mol%)**: Increases polarity, H-bonding
- **Methanol (2–10 mol%)**: Similar to ethanol, more polar
- **Water (1–5 mol%)**: Dramatically increases polarity
- **Acetone**: For specific compound classes

**Effect on Solubility Parameter (δ)**:

Pure CO₂ at 40°C, 20 MPa: δ ≈ 12 MPa^(1/2) (similar to hexane)
CO₂ + 10% ethanol: δ ≈ 15–16 MPa^(1/2) (similar to toluene)

**Trade-offs**:
- ✓ Improved solvation of polar compounds
- ✓ Reduced extraction time
- ✗ Modifier must be separated from product
- ✗ Lower selectivity (co-extraction of undesired compounds)

### Environmental and Safety Considerations

| Factor | CO₂ | Water | Ethanol | Propane |
|--------|-----|-------|---------|---------|
| **Toxicity** | None | None | Low | Low |
| **Flammability** | None | None | Flammable | Highly Flammable |
| **Environmental Impact** | Very Low (natural) | None | Low (renewable) | Moderate (GHG) |
| **Waste Disposal** | Recycle or vent | Discharge (if clean) | Recycle | Must contain |
| **Regulatory Approval** | GRAS, widely approved | Approved | Food-grade available | Restricted (flammable) |

**Safety Hierarchy (Industrial Preference)**:
1. **CO₂** – First choice for most applications
2. **Water/Ethanol** – When polarity or temperature required
3. **Propane/Nitrogen** – Specialized applications with safety controls
4. **Fluorinated/Xenon** – Only when no alternative exists

### Code 2: CO₂ Density Calculator Using Equation of State

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

class CO2_EOS:
    """Simplified Peng-Robinson equation of state for CO₂ density"""

    def __init__(self):
        # CO₂ properties
        self.Tc = 304.13  # K
        self.Pc = 7.3773e6  # Pa
        self.omega = 0.225  # acentric factor
        self.R = 8.314  # J/(mol·K)
        self.MW = 0.04401  # kg/mol

        # Peng-Robinson parameters
        self.a = 0.45724 * (self.R**2 * self.Tc**2) / self.Pc
        self.b = 0.07780 * (self.R * self.Tc) / self.Pc

    def alpha(self, T):
        """Temperature-dependent parameter"""
        Tr = T / self.Tc
        kappa = 0.37464 + 1.54226*self.omega - 0.26992*self.omega**2
        return (1 + kappa*(1 - np.sqrt(Tr)))**2

    def Z_factor(self, T, P):
        """Compressibility factor Z = PV/(nRT)"""
        A = self.a * self.alpha(T) * P / (self.R**2 * T**2)
        B = self.b * P / (self.R * T)

        # Cubic equation: Z³ - (1-B)Z² + (A-3B²-2B)Z - (AB-B²-B³) = 0
        coeffs = [1,
                  -(1 - B),
                  A - 3*B**2 - 2*B,
                  -(A*B - B**2 - B**3)]

        roots = np.roots(coeffs)
        # Take largest real root for vapor/supercritical phase
        real_roots = roots[np.isreal(roots)].real
        return np.max(real_roots) if len(real_roots) > 0 else 1.0

    def density(self, T_C, P_MPa):
        """Calculate CO₂ density in kg/m³"""
        T = T_C + 273.15  # Convert to K
        P = P_MPa * 1e6  # Convert to Pa

        Z = self.Z_factor(T, P)
        # Molar volume: V = ZRT/P
        V_molar = Z * self.R * T / P  # m³/mol
        # Density: ρ = MW/V_molar
        rho = self.MW / V_molar  # kg/m³

        return rho

# Create EOS object
co2 = CO2_EOS()

print("-" * 70)

conditions = [

    (40, 20, "Supercritical"),
```

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
from scipy.interpolate import griddata

def water_dielectric_constant(T_C, P_MPa):
    """
    Calculate water dielectric constant using empirical correlation
    Based on Bradley & Pitzer (1979) and IAPWS formulations

    Valid range: 0–800°C, 0.1–100 MPa
    """
    T_K = T_C + 273.15
    rho = water_density_approx(T_C, P_MPa)  # kg/m³

    # Simplified empirical formula
    # ε = 1 + A·ρ + B·ρ² / T
    # Parameters fitted to IAPWS data
    A = 7.62571e-3
    B = 244.003

    epsilon = 1 + A * rho + B * rho**2 / T_K

    return max(1.0, epsilon)  # Minimum value is 1 (vacuum)

def water_density_approx(T_C, P_MPa):
    """
    Approximate water density using simple correlation
    Not accurate near critical point, but sufficient for demonstration
    """
    T_K = T_C + 273.15
    Tc = 647.1  # K
    Pc = 22.064  # MPa
    rho_c = 322  # kg/m³

    if T_C < 100:
        # Liquid region approximation
        rho = 1000 * (1 - 0.0002 * (T_C - 25))
    else:
        # Supercritical/high-T approximation
        Tr = T_K / Tc
        Pr = P_MPa / Pc
        # Very crude approximation
        rho = rho_c * (1 + 2*Pr) / (1 + Tr)

    return max(10, rho)  # Minimum density
```

```python
                'Tc_C': 96.7, 'Pc_MPa': 4.25, 'polarity': 'low',
                'safety': 'flammable', 'cost': 'low', 'flammable': True
            },
            'Nitrogen': {
                'Tc_C': -147, 'Pc_MPa': 3.4, 'polarity': 'very_low',
                'safety': 'excellent', 'cost': 'low', 'flammable': False
            }
        }

    def recommend(self, max_temp=None, polarity=None,
                  allow_flammable=False, budget=None):
        """
        Recommend suitable SCF based on criteria

        Parameters:
        -----------
        max_temp : float
            Maximum allowable temperature (°C) for target compound
        polarity : str
            Required polarity ('low', 'medium', 'high')
        allow_flammable : bool
            Whether flammable solvents are acceptable
        budget : str
            Cost constraint ('low', 'medium', 'high', 'unlimited')

        Returns:
        --------
        ranked list of suitable SCFs with scores
        """
        results = []

        for scf, props in self.scf_db.items():
            score = 100
            reasons = []

            # Temperature check
            if max_temp is not None:
                if props['Tc_C'] > max_temp:
                    score -= 50
                    reasons.append(f"Tc too high ({props['Tc_C']}°C > {max_temp}°C)")
                else:
                    margin = max_temp - props['Tc_C']
                    score += min(20, margin / 5)  # Bonus for safety margin
                    reasons.append(f"Good temp margin ({margin:.0f}°C)")

            # Polarity match
            if polarity is not None:
                polarity_match = {
                    'low': ['low', 'very_low'],
                    'medium': ['medium', 'low', 'high_to_low'],
                    'high': ['high_to_low', 'medium']
                }
                if props['polarity'] in polarity_match.get(polarity, []):
                    score += 20
                    reasons.append("Polarity match")
                else:
                    score -= 30
                    reasons.append("Polarity mismatch")

            # Flammability check
            if not allow_flammable and props['flammable']:
                score -= 40
                reasons.append("Flammable (not allowed)")

            # Cost consideration
            if budget == 'low' and props['cost'] in ['very_low', 'low']:
                score += 10
                reasons.append("Cost-effective")
            elif budget == 'low' and props['cost'] in ['medium', 'high']:
                score -= 20
                reasons.append("Over budget")

            # Safety bonus
            if props['safety'] == 'excellent':
                score += 15
                reasons.append("Excellent safety")

            results.append({
                'SCF': scf,
                'Score': max(0, score),
                'Tc_C': props['Tc_C'],
                'Pc_MPa': props['Pc_MPa'],
                'Polarity': props['polarity'],
                'Safety': props['safety'],
                'Reasons': '; '.join(reasons)
            })

        # Sort by score
        results = sorted(results, key=lambda x: x['Score'], reverse=True)

        return pd.DataFrame(results)
```

### Code 5: Modifier Effect on Solubility Parameter

```python
import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def hildebrand_solubility_parameter(fluid, T_C, P_MPa, modifier_mol=0):
    """
    Calculate Hildebrand solubility parameter (δ) in MPa^0.5

    δ = sqrt(ΔHvap / V_molar) for liquids
    For SCFs, approximate using density and molecular properties

    Parameters:
    -----------
    fluid : str ('CO2', 'ethanol', 'water')
    T_C : float (temperature in °C)
    P_MPa : float (pressure in MPa)
    modifier_mol : float (mol% modifier, default 0)
    """

    # Reference solubility parameters (MPa^0.5) at standard conditions
    delta_ref = {
        'CO2': 12.0,      # Non-polar, similar to hexane
        'ethanol': 26.5,  # Polar, H-bonding
        'water': 47.8,    # Very polar, strong H-bonding
        'hexane': 14.9,
        'toluene': 18.0,
        'acetone': 19.9
    }

    if fluid == 'CO2':
        # Pure CO₂ correlation
        # δ increases with density/pressure
        delta_base = delta_ref['CO2'] * (1 + 0.03 * (P_MPa - 10))

        # Modifier effect (linear mixing approximation)
        if modifier_mol > 0:
            delta_mod = delta_ref['ethanol']  # Assume ethanol modifier
            delta = delta_base + (delta_mod - delta_base) * (modifier_mol / 100)
        else:
            delta = delta_base

    elif fluid == 'water':
        # Water δ decreases with temperature above Tc
        T_K = T_C + 273.15
        Tc_water = 647.1  # K
        if T_K < Tc_water:
            delta = delta_ref['water'] * (1 - 0.1 * (T_K - 298) / 100)
        else:
            # Dramatic decrease in supercritical region
            delta = delta_ref['water'] * 0.2 * (Tc_water / T_K)**2

    elif fluid == 'ethanol':
        delta = delta_ref['ethanol'] * (1 - 0.05 * (T_C - 25) / 100)

    else:
        delta = 15.0  # Default

    return max(5.0, delta)  # Minimum value
```

## Summary

This chapter covered the properties and applications of common supercritical fluids:

1. **Supercritical CO₂** is the dominant industrial SCF due to:
   - Mild critical conditions (31.1°C, 7.38 MPa)
   - Excellent safety profile (non-toxic, non-flammable)
   - Low cost and easy separation
   - Tunable properties via pressure and modifiers

2. **Supercritical Water** exhibits extreme property changes:
   - Dielectric constant drops from 78 → ~2 above Tc
   - Becomes non-polar, dissolving organics
   - Enables SCWO for hazardous waste destruction
   - Requires high-pressure equipment (22 MPa)

3. **Supercritical Ethanol** offers:
   - Intermediate polarity (versatile)
   - Green solvent alternative (renewable)
   - Applications in biodiesel and biomass processing
   - Moderate critical point (241°C, 6 MPa)

4. **Other SCFs** (propane, nitrogen, xenon) have specialized applications but are less common due to flammability, extreme conditions, or cost.

5. **Solvent selection** depends on:
   - Target compound thermal stability (→ Tc requirement)
   - Polarity matching (with or without modifiers)
   - Safety constraints (flammability, toxicity)
   - Economic considerations (capital, operating costs)

**Key Takeaway**: CO₂ is the default choice for 80% of SCF applications. Use water for oxidation/synthesis at high temperatures, ethanol for biodiesel/biomass, and others only for specialized needs.
